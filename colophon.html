How this site is built

I wanted a place that could hold my artwork, education work, technical curiosity, and everyday interests together. A café gallery felt closer to that than a job title and a polished portrait.

A corner of a café

The Leaf Café in Đà Nẵng is the reference: warm plaster, dark wood, greenery, and small tile details. I borrowed its atmosphere without using my photographs of it as decoration, and my artwork keeps its own colors.

The homepage is laid out like a corner of the café. A dark ceiling with small downlights holds the navigation, my name sits on the plaster wall, the work lies on a table under the trees, and my contact details are on the serving counter at the bottom. Each project is its own paper, some folded like pamphlets, spread out loosely the way you’d lay work on a table to look through it. Click a paper to read its story. The paper is textured after giấy dó, the Vietnamese paper made from the inner bark of the dó tree and formed on a bamboo screen: fine bark fibres, the screen’s faint lines, and the odd fleck of bark. Jacaranda and bougainvillea hang over the table; I chose them for their connection to Đà Nẵng and my Vietnamese heritage. The jacaranda is cut from my own photo of the café’s skylight and the bougainvillea comes from a painting; both step aside on small screens so the work has room.

The wall lamp is a trombone slide turned into a light, drawn after one at the café; pull its cord to switch it. On the counter there is an espresso machine you can pull a shot from, an iced matcha latte like the one I ordered there, egg coffee kept warm in a bowl of hot water, and chè sương sa hạt lựu with pandan jelly and red water-chestnut pieces. Each drink can be sipped and refilled. The counter ends in a lift-up flap and a gate that swings. If you stay a few minutes, the coffee stops steaming and the matcha glass fogs up.

Useful before the first click

The introduction says what I do and how to reach me, and each piece of work says what I made before you open anything. Longer stories, process, and personal interests are one click deeper for anyone who wants them.

Typography with a story

My typography class made me pay closer attention to how type shapes reading. I chose Bricolage Grotesque partly because of its story. Designer Mathieu Triay connects its French and British influences to migration and belonging. That story resonated with me; its expressive shapes also bring character to the page.

Bricolage sets nearly everything, from my name to the paragraphs, so one voice runs across all of my work. Fraunces, by Phaedra Charles and Flavia Zimbardi, appears at a few reflective moments: the note on AI and the openings of two stories. I compared several serif and sans-serif faces for the paragraphs before settling on Bricolage.

Both fonts are hosted with the site under the SIL Open Font License (Bricolage license; Fraunces license), including the characters Vietnamese needs.

How it works

It is a small static site: HTML, CSS, and three short scripts, with no framework or build step, hosted on GitHub Pages. There are no analytics, third-party scripts, remote fonts, or cookies.

A café that follows the day

The light follows your clock, and I lit it the way a painter would: warm light against cool shade. Dawn starts lavender and turns rose, midday is clean and pale, low sun turns orange with violet shadows, and night is a cool blue room warmed by the lamps. The sun rises on the left and sets on the right, moving the patches of sun and the papers’ shadows. When the light is low, the edge of each paper facing it catches a thin highlight, and the patches of sun glow a little.

The sunlight is made the way real dappled light works: every gap between leaves projects a small oval image of the sun, and where gaps crowd together, the ovals merge into bright patches with scalloped edges. The light stays where it falls while the papers slide under it as you scroll. You can scrub through the day with the slider, and the Eye candy switch stops all the ambient motion.

Built to be usable

Everything you can interact with is a real link or button, so it can all be reached and used from the keyboard. Stories open as dialogs, Esc closes them, and focus returns to where you were. Reduced-motion settings turn off the swaying and transitions, high-contrast modes remove the decoration, and without JavaScript everything stays readable at midday. The seal comparison sweeps across once when it scrolls into view, unless you have already touched it or asked for reduced motion. The heatmap clip plays silently when it scrolls into view and can be paused; the other clips play only when you ask.

The illustration’s process frames come from my timelapse, and the game footage comes from my running prototype. Captions explain what each recording shows.

Checks

Before I publish, a small script checks links, IDs, and file sizes. I test the page at desktop, tablet, and phone sizes, at different times of day, with the keyboard, with reduced motion, and with JavaScript off, and I check that text stays readable as the light moves across it. I haven’t tested with screen readers on real devices yet.

I set the direction, chose the references, and reviewed every change; I built the site with AI assistance for code and writing. The jacaranda over the table is cut from my own photo looking up through the café’s skylight. The bougainvillea and the leaf shapes in the sunlight come from AI-generated paintings, and the wood grain, paper texture, and light patterns are generated in code. They are decoration, not my artwork.
